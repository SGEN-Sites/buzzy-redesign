What Is Hash? Effects & Where to Buy in Brooklyn

August 204 min read

N° 36 / THE BUZZY GUIDE

Hash, short for hashish, is a concentrated form of cannabis made by separating and compressing trichomes, the resin glands that coat the cannabis flower. Because it’s almost pure resin, hash is significantly more potent than flower, with THC levels that typically run from the 40s up into the 70-80% range, compared to 15-25% for flower.

What’s the Difference Between Hash and Hashish?

Functionally, none, “hash” is just the shortened, modern name for hashish. The word hashish comes from Arabic and has been used for over a thousand years to describe cannabis resin that’s been collected and pressed into a solid mass. What’s changed is technique: traditional hashish was hand-rubbed from live plants (charas) or sieved from dried flower and pressed by hand, a method still practiced in regions like Morocco, Lebanon, and Afghanistan. Today, most hash sold in U.S. dispensaries is made with cleaner, more controlled solventless methods, but it’s the same basic idea: isolate the resin, leave the plant matter behind.

How Is Hash Made?

There are a few different roads to the same destination, a concentrated block, powder, or paste of trichomes:

  • Dry sift: Dried flower is gently rubbed over fine mesh screens, knocking loose trichome heads (kief) without any water or solvent. Sifted through progressively finer screens, it can be pressed into blocks or used as-is.
  • Bubble hash (ice water hash): Fresh-frozen or dried flower is agitated in ice water, which makes the brittle trichomes snap off and sink. The resin is then filtered through a series of micron mesh bags, rinsed, and dried. This is the most common method behind today’s premium solventless hash.
  • Rosin: Bubble hash or dry sift is pressed between heated plates, and heat and pressure squeeze the resin out with no solvents involved. This is often the most potent and flavorful step in the process, since it starts from an already-concentrated material.
  • Temple balls & charas: Traditional, hand-worked styles, bubble hash rolled and cured into balls, or fresh resin hand-rubbed straight from the live plant, that lean on time and technique rather than mechanical processing.

All of these are considered solventless, no butane, CO2, or other chemical solvents involved, just physical separation using screens, ice water, heat, and pressure.

How Strong Is Hash Compared to Flower?

Ranges vary by starting material, plant genetics, and processing quality, the number on the label is always the most accurate read for a specific product.

How Do You Use Hash?

Hash is flexible. Common ways people use it:

  • Crumbled into a bowl or joint and smoked alongside flower, the traditional method, and still the most common
  • Dabbed on a low-temp rig, especially full-melt bubble hash or rosin, which vaporize cleanly at lower temperatures than most other concentrates
  • Pressed further into rosin for an even more concentrated, flavor-forward product
  • Rolled into a pre-roll for a no-equipment-needed way to try hash, no dab rig or grinder required

Because hash is so much stronger than flower, a little goes a long way, especially for anyone who hasn’t used concentrates before.

Understanding the Production Methods of Cannabis Hash

Is hash the same thing as hashish?

Yes. “Hash” is simply the shortened, everyday name for hashish, both refer to concentrated cannabis resin separated from the flower.

Is hash stronger than regular weed?

Generally, yes. Because hash is concentrated trichomes rather than whole flower, it typically tests well above flower’s THC range, often 2-3x stronger, depending on the grade.

How do you smoke hash?

Most people crumble it into a joint or bowl with flower, or dab higher-grade bubble hash and rosin on a low-temp rig for a cleaner, more flavorful hit. Hash-infused pre-rolls are also an easy, no-equipment way to try it.

Is hash legal to buy in New York?

Yes, hash and other concentrates are legal for adults 21+ to purchase at licensed New York dispensaries like BUZZY. Ask your budtender about current purchase limits.

Is hash a good choice for a first-time concentrate user?

It can be, especially dry sift, a lower-potency bubble hash, or a hash-infused pre-roll, since these are easier to dose than a straight dab of rosin. Start small and go slow, you can always take more, but you can’t take less.

How should hash be stored?

Keep it cool, dark, and airtight, a small glass jar in a drawer works well. Heat and light break down terpenes and potency over time.
